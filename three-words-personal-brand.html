The 3 Words That Change How People See You

A tiny vocabulary exercise with an outsized effect on how rooms, feeds and first meetings receive you.

Practice 5 min read 05 Oct 2026
The short answer

Your three brand words are the three feelings people should take away from meeting you — chosen deliberately from your archetype, strengths and aspirations, then applied to your LinkedIn headline, introductions and content. They keep your presence consistent. The free Rise Personal Snapshot generates your three words in about four minutes.

Here is an uncomfortable question: if ten people who met you this month each wrote down three words to describe you, would their lists match? For most professionals, they wouldn’t — not because the impressions were bad, but because they were random. A scattered brand forces every new acquaintance to guess at who you are. Three well-chosen words end the guessing.

One sentence, three words

The exercise is simple to state. Complete this sentence: “When people meet my brand, they should feel ______, ______ and ______.” Not “think of” — feel. Perception is emotional before it is rational; the adjectives arrive before the CV does.

Three is the number that works. One word is a slogan, two is a comparison, four is a list. Three words fit in memory, fit in a headline, and force genuine choice — which is exactly the point.

How to choose your three

Random beautiful words won’t survive contact with a real week. Durable words come from three lines that cross:

Your archetype — the feel

Your leading brand archetype sets the emotional register: a Sage leads with “lucid,” a Caregiver with “safe,” a Hero with “driven.” Work with the grain of how people already receive you, not against it.

Your strengths — the proof

Which qualities do colleagues actually rely on — precision, warmth, nerve, rigour? Words you can evidence in every project read as true; words you merely aspire to read as decoration.

Your aspiration — the direction

One word may come from where you are going, not where you are — “bold” for the quiet expert stepping onto bigger stages. Aspiration keeps the trio alive as you grow.

What three words look like

Feeling is easier with examples. Notice how each trio does quiet positioning work:

Calm. Precise. Caring.

A private physician in Dubai — authority without coldness, in a market where trust is everything.

Bold. Candid. Grounded.

A founder raising capital — the confidence investors look for, anchored by substance.

Curious. Vivid. Considered.

A creative director — imagination with discipline, in three beats.

Clear. Warm. Unflinching.

An executive coach — someone who tells you the truth kindly and doesn’t blink.

Put the words to work

Chosen words earn their keep only when applied — and the applications are wonderfully practical.

  • Your LinkedIn headline. Work the words into the line under your name, and let the trio — not job-title boilerplate — do the describing.
  • Your introduction. Rehearse a two-line intro that lands the three feelings. Rooms remember feelings that arrived on time.
  • Your content filter. Before publishing anything, ask: does this make my three words more true in the reader’s mind? If not, rewrite or drop it.

Used this way, three small words start acting like a strategy: they align your headline, your handshake and your feed — the whole of your presence pulling in one direction.

Keeping the words honest

Words drift. Six months of hard projects can quietly earn you a fourth word you never chose — “stretched,” “distant,” “fierce.” So build two small maintenance habits. First, the monthly echo: ask two people who met you recently what stood out about you, and listen for your three words — or their absence. Second, the yearly review: as you grow, one word may retire and another may ask to join. That is not inconsistency; that is calibration. The trio is a living instrument, tuned to the person you actually are this season — which is why we pair the exercise with a wellness practice rather than a one-off workshop.

A small warning from experience: don’t choose words for the market you wish would notice you. Choose them for the people you actually serve. Words aimed at everyone are felt by no one; words aimed with love at a specific room echo for years.
